# Interaction plan

Current flow: a narrated test starts automatically after calibration, with manual controls available when **Narrated test after calibration** is disabled. Delivery remains Windows Chrome -> SteamVR/OpenXR -> Steam Link -> Quest 3. The scan placement in [scanAsset.ts](../src/viewer/scanAsset.ts) is user-adjusted and protected: changing this flow does not change that file or the calibration landmarks.

## Narrated test

Timing starts at the successful fit callback, during the existing one-second scan reveal. In live VR, the first box appears at +10 seconds and waits for headset entry. The second box appears five seconds after turn onset. The wall remains hidden until **two seconds after the actual four-second rotation finishes**, then slides in at **hallway route Z=-8** and approaches **hallway route Z=-2** at **0.30 m/s**. The wall pauses for a nearby player and never accelerates to meet a narration deadline. The desktop narrated preview retains its 10/15/20-second box/turn/seat sequence, with wall entry at approximately +21 seconds.

| Time after calibration | Action and narration |
| --- | --- |
| 0 s | “welcome, I hope you are calibrated to your new environment” |
| 10 s | Show first box: “well would you look at that, a box appeared... I wonder if it's real...” |
| Headset inside or above shown first box | Start the four-second turn: “Whoosh, what just happened?” |
| Five seconds after turn onset | Reveal second box: “oh look there's your seat because you are late for class.” |
| One second after rotation completes | First box glitches out in place over 0.55 seconds; its logical pivot stays fixed. |
| Two seconds after rotation completes | Reveal wall at hallway route Z=-8; approach -2 only while the player is clear. |

The clock calls the same guarded placement/reveal actions as the buttons. Manual action and placement controls wait while the test runs; STOP and Reset remain available. A failed placement, stalled frames, new calibration, changed scan placement, VR session change or tracking/focus loss cancels further actions and audio. Recovery requires a fresh calibration. Disabling the test cancels it and restores manual controls. **Preview narrated test on desktop** resets the visual baseline and runs without granting a physical fit. See [voice setup](VOICE_SETUP.md) for required local ElevenLabs clips and access troubleshooting.

## Operator sequence

Both boxes use the supplied `public/splats/WoodenBox.ply`, preloaded hidden in the hallway's shared renderer. Their source orientation is corrected upright. **Show box** and **Show second box** rise from below their floor over 1.5 seconds with ease in/out; manual **Hide box** and **Hide second box** descend beneath it over the same duration. Both standing bases are on their logical floor anchors with no extra visual lift. The floor clips the submerged splats. Buttons wait for a transition to finish; a hidden or rising first box cannot trigger headset entry. One second after the actual turn completes, the first box automatically glitches out over 0.55 seconds: horizontal bands jitter and chunks disappear at its standing pose, then its scene hides. This automatic exit never lowers the visible box or moves its anchor/pivot. It also runs with manual turns and narration disabled. Explicit Show can restore the box afterward; recovery cancels pending/active effects. The second box follows the current fitted wall at its prepared longitudinal station and stays visible until manual Hide or recovery.

1. Load the hallway scan. For a headset run, enter VR and complete the existing controller calibration first. Desktop use requires neither a headset nor a calibration fit.
2. Click **Show box** in Chrome. The app estimates the hallway centre at the selected cross-section and shows the first upright **0.36 x 0.36 x 0.43 m** box with its bottom on **local-floor Y = 0**, matching the cyan origin/grid reference. It also prepares the second box's placement while keeping that box hidden.
3. Review the centering and second-box placement messages. If the first wall fit is weak, use **Pick two floor edges**; their midpoint supplies X/Z on floor Y = 0. To refine the second box's approximate guide, open placement details and choose **Pick second-box floor line**, then click its visible floor line once in the PC view before rotating.
4. Click **Rotate hallway 180°** when ready. The second placement's longitudinal station and physical wall side are prepared before the turn. The visual hallway turns smoothly over **four seconds** around the first box's vertical axis. The first box and tracked camera, hands and controllers remain fixed in their original XR frame.
5. When the turn finishes, click **Show second box**. This separate button refits that station against the current rotated hallway and places one whole footprint edge on the selected fitted wall. In the live narrated test the same action runs five seconds after turn onset; desktop preview uses +20 seconds.
6. **STOP** freezes a turn and invalidates live alignment. **Reset baseline** restores the saved hallway transform. Reset, fresh calibration, stop and session changes clear the prepared/visible second box. Headset use requires fresh calibration after an interruption or operator stop; resetting alone does not restore acceptance.

For the wall mismatch after flipping, open **Flipped hallway wall alignment**. The starting trial value is **+10 cm**, shifting right when facing down the original hallway; use negative values for left. Apply a number or nudge by 1 cm. The shift eases in during the turn, leaving the original calibrated view and first box fixed. The broom, floor opening and second box use the corrected hallway's current fitted sections at their explicit world-Z stations and follow the eased offset. The wall fits its whole route once before entry and keeps that trajectory fixed through subsequent corrections; hide/show fits a new route. Normal gameplay keeps the wall and second box hidden through the turn. After the turn and narrated test finish, further adjustments preview immediately. **Zero** removes the correction. Mid-turn/STOP/tracking interruptions block edits. Reset restores the original scan transform and retains only the page's chosen draft amount for the next turn. A local shift cannot correct differing hallway widths or wall angles; verify both real walls before treating the reversed view as aligned.

Both wooden-box appearances sit on the floor with no added height offset. Their dimensions remain metric; refitting the second box does not move the first box's trigger volume or pivot.

With the narrated test disabled, Show box and Show second box remain operator actions. The shown first box still triggers a turn from headset position; Rotate hallway is also available. There is no orb collection or support/placement checkbox. A completed turn requires reset before another turn. Both boxes default to **0.36 x 0.36 x 0.43 m**; the first box's dimensions remain editable, while the second box keeps those fixed dimensions.

## Lights out

The PC **Lights out / Lights on** button fades all scanned hallway/prop colors between normal brightness and the selected dark-mode brightness over 0.65 seconds. The default is **30% brightness**. Use the **Dark-mode brightness** slider from **0% (black)** to **100% (normal)** to choose the manual effect; the percentage appears beside it. While lights are out, adjustments preview immediately. With lights on, adjustments only configure the next manual activation. The browser remembers the manually chosen brightness through refresh and recovery; activation and alignment are never restored from this preference. Lighting is baked into these splats, so the effect multiplies rendered RGB; it does not change opacity, calibration fades, geometry or scan placement. Calibration/recovery instructions and tracked hands retain their normal appearance.

Two seconds after the second box finishes its floor rise, the lights flicker for one second with irregular, diminishing relights and settle at exactly **30% brightness**. This cue follows actual settled visibility in both manual and narrated paths, rather than a guessed scenario deadline. The original local `public/audio/light_flicker.mp3` is preloaded/decoded from a page or Enter VR gesture; only its first second plays, at16% gain, with an80ms fade-in and200ms fade-out. This sound is independent of narration. A missing/blocked sound cannot delay the visual cue or play late. Hiding the box before the deadline cancels the pending cue. Manual lighting/brightness commands override the automatic cue for that appearance.

The system control is `hallwayLighting.setLightsOut(true)` / `hallwayLighting.setLightsOut(false)` on the instance created in [main.ts](../src/main.ts); it returns whether the command was accepted. `setLightsOutBrightness(value)` accepts a finite value from0 to1 and updates an active preview immediately. Repeated activation commands do not restart a fade, and reversing direction starts from the current brightness. The instance is also available through the existing local `window.__debug.hallwayLighting` hook. There is no controller, hand or direct headset-position lighting binding; the slider supports standard PC keyboard adjustment when focused. The PC operator/system can use it during a turn.

It starts with lights on. Activation waits for scene readiness and, in live XR, a current calibration and visible headset tracking. Turning lights on remains available for recovery. STOP, Reset baseline, calibration preparation, new/ended sessions and tracking/reference-space/focus interruptions immediately restore normal brightness, stop the sound and cancel pending/active fades or flicker; later frames cannot revive that appearance's cue. A genuinely new second-box reveal can arm a fresh cue. The shared shader is prepared before startup readiness, and toggles reuse existing render resources. Headset sound level, stereo appearance and comfort remain separate hardware checks.

## Extracted wall object

Wall route coordinates match desktop world coordinates. A successful live calibration maps them through the session's hallway yaw and horizontal origin, frozen before the artistic turn, so SteamVR's tracking axes cannot swap the approach side. Floor Y stays zero. Player poses and entry/clearance checks use this same route frame; displayed and manually entered XYZ stay in actual world metres. Every new fit replaces the frame, including with narration disabled; recovery still requires fresh calibration.

The hallway and Git LFS tracked uncompressed `public/splats/wall-section/wall-section.ply` load together in one startup batch. The wall stays hidden, its shader is warmed, and operator controls wait until both assets are ready. The default **Show wall** slides it from the left to **hallway route Z=-8**, using a route fitted once from five supported sections between -8 and -2. A straight centreline supplies one constant heading, and one width covers both walls over the sampled route with 1 cm edge overlap for scan noise. It then approaches -2 at 0.30 m/s without any further section queries, changes in width/orientation, or following later scan corrections. This prevents jumps between section fits. Its next reveal or explicit authoring action fits again. Gameplay arms this reveal at calibration/preview start, waits for the actual completed turn plus two seconds, then shows it if the player is clear. A second-box cue never forces arrival. **Hide wall** continues the side slide to the right until hidden. Each uninterrupted side transition takes1.25seconds. Toggles reuse the preloaded geometry, textures, material and sorter. A weak opposing-wall fit leaves the object hidden and reports the reason.

The automatic route has hard hallway route Z bounds **[-8,-2]** and only increases Z. Its full-thickness horizontal footprint uses the actual fitted/rendered width and keeps **75cm** clearance from the current valid headset position, with **15cm** extra clearance before resuming. The wall waits for a player on either side; each step is limited before it can cross the player's buffer, using the next point on the locked line and its unchanged rendered width. Waiting does not accumulate catch-up distance. Its side-entry sweep also pauses or stays hidden if occupied; missing current XR tracking blocks progress. Desktop preview uses the inspection camera as the player. Both side slides keep their centre at the same hallway route Z even when calibration yaw is nonzero. These are visual proximity guards based on head position, not a physical collision barrier or certified body clearance.

Open **Wall position & motion** to edit the wall's bottom-centre **X/Y/Z in current world metres**, then click **Set wall position**. Its live coordinate appears above the controls. Placement works hidden or visible once the side slide finishes; it never shows a hidden wall automatically. **Use start position** restores Z=-8. Explicit position/movement/Stop commands override the scripted approach and cancel a pending automatic reveal. Manual coordinates last for this page and stay in scan space, so hide/show retains them and a fresh rigid calibration reprojects them. Starting a fresh gameplay sequence arms a new fitted post-turn reveal at -8. These controls do not grant physical alignment acceptance.

**Move forward** travels away from the hallway start; **Move back** travels toward it. Each moves by **Travel** at the selected **Speed**, then stops. **Back & forth** repeats smoothly between plus/minus Travel around its starting point, with Speed as the maximum speed. Defaults are **1 m** and **0.15 m/s**; travel is limited to 10 m and speed to 1 m/s. **Stop motion** freezes the current position and makes it the next standing coordinate. Hide also stops forward/back motion there before sliding rightward. Starting another motion begins at the current location. Reset, calibration, STOP and session/tracking recovery cancel movement and hide the wall; no motion resumes automatically. These controls reuse the already loaded wall, leaving hallway/rig transforms and both boxes untouched.

The object copies 33,437 original splats from the door-free graffiti wall near raw X = 0.90, Y = 19.75–21.85. This is the densest 2.10 m interval on a 0.10 m search grid within the visually clean wall stretch Y = 16.25–22.75. The wall height is 2.60 m, retaining the continuous scanned wall from raw Z = -1.45 to +1.15, including the upper wall above the horizontal band at +0.95. These are scan-derived dimensions; physical metric validation remains separate. Positions, normals and Gaussian orientation are rotated into a bottom-centred object frame; colour, scale and opacity remain original with no resampling or compression. Recreate the local asset with `npm run scan:extract-wall`; an existing crop requires the explicit `-- --force` option. Source fingerprint and crop evidence are included in the adjacent tracked metadata. Source/crop and placement settings live in [wallSectionConfig.ts](../src/viewer/wallSectionConfig.ts), independently of the protected manual scan placement.

Both outward sides show the good scanned face. A second full-thickness copy is rotated 180 degrees about up and baked into the same asset (66,874 splats total). The two 24 cm slabs have a 2 cm gap, making the authored total depth 50 cm; original Gaussian scales are preserved. Both faces share one scene and bottom-centre coordinate, so every slide, travel command and gameplay animation moves them together. Recreate an older single-face derivative with `npm run scan:extract-wall -- --force`.

The copy shares the hallway's splat sorter so foreground/background blending respects depth. A scene-specific rectangular shader clip keeps visible Gaussian tails within each authored 2.10 ×2.60 m face at its own depth. X scale is set once to span both walls across the fitted route and full double-faced slab; its 2.60 m height and 50 cm depth remain unchanged. Per-eye inward-face culling prevents the poor reverse side from blending through the outward good copy. During the scripted -8-to--2 route, X is evaluated on the frozen straight line; heading and width remain constant. World tracking axes and the first box remain fixed. Source-only centres remain the input to landmark/prop fits; the wall never reads those sections after locking its route. Reset, calibration, stop and tracking/session interruptions immediately cancel pending reveals and hide/cancel the wall. A late completed-turn callback cannot revive an interrupted cue. A new enabled gameplay sequence after fresh calibration is an explicit new start. The wall is a visual obstruction and adds no physical collision geometry.

Model equality is numerical; scan appearance residuals still limit physical accuracy.

The box's horizontal edges align with the fitted world-space hallway direction. Two-edge placement uses the perpendicular of the picked wall-to-wall vector. A new calibration updates this heading once; box orientation stays fixed while the visual hallway turns. This changes neither the floor centre nor the turn pivot.

Report the fit result on the operator panel. A weak or ambiguous fit leaves the two-floor-edge picker as the explicit fallback. ## Coordinate and lifecycle contract

- Keep one XR unit = one metre, with the tracked camera/controller/hand parents at identity. Apply calibration and the artistic turn to the scan root only. Preserve the user-adjusted scan normalization and the separate A/B landmarks.
- Both boxes remain upright and metric in XR space. Each mesh centre is half its height above its floor contact. The first box alone supplies the turn pivot; preparing or revealing the second box does not change that pivot.
- Save the current calibrated scan transform before the turn. Compute each animated pose from that baseline and the current yaw, keeping the floor level and finishing at exactly 180 degrees without compounding transforms.
- A new fit, scan/start-point change, new session, reference-space reset, focus interruption, missing/emulated viewer tracking, or operator stop removes live alignment authority. Restore the baseline before another calibration. Recovery stays explicit; there is no automatic re-solving or persisted acceptance.
- STOP freezes the current visual pose. Reset is an explicit restoration while the wearer is still under operator guidance. Loss of an unused hand/controller alone does not invalidate hallway alignment.

The 180-degree reversal is an artistic divergence from calibrated alignment. An asymmetric reversed hallway may no longer match its real walls. Independent checks are required before further wall-touch exploration. The first physical box/turn trials remain supervised; desktop screenshots cannot establish spatial accuracy or comfort.

## Validation

Automated checks cover manual transitions and the separate +0/+10/+15/+20 desktop narrated clock, hidden preparation and post-turn refitting/reveal, the exact four-second yaw, fixed first-box/tracked parents, wall-adjacent second-box placement, current-section prop alignment, baseline restoration and interruption invalidation. ## Headset box trigger

The first box is a one-shot trigger while shown in a calibrated live session. Its fixed world-space, yaw-aligned footprint extends upward from its base without a height ceiling: the headset can be inside the box or directly above it. Sideways proximity and positions below the base do not count. This measures head position, not foot contact or physical support. Only a finite, non-emulated pose from the current visible XR frame can trigger; desktop camera motion cannot. Calibration, tracking/focus loss, STOP and reset keep their existing guards. The trigger uses the shown placement without re-fitting or moving the pivot. The four-second turn retains smoothstep ease-in/out with zero endpoint speed, and the first box and tracked rig remain fixed. Completing a turn cannot retrigger until reset and Show box.
